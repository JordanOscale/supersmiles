In plain English

You may be eligible if…

If a few of these sound like you, it's worth a free check. We'll confirm the details honestly.

  • You've been told you need major dental work.
  • You're in dental pain, discomfort or distress.
  • You (or a dependent) have no other way to pay.
  • You have some funds in your super.
  • You can get a GP letter and an itemised dental quote — we help with both.

Often qualifies (when medically necessary)

ImplantsCrownsRoot canals ExtractionsOral surgeryReconstructive work

Usually doesn't qualify

WhiteningVeneers (cosmetic)Purely cosmetic work

…unless there's a genuine medical purpose, e.g. restoring function after trauma.

What the ATO needs

Three things — and we help with all of them.

To approve a compassionate release for dental, the ATO looks for these. You don't have to gather them alone.

An itemised dental quote

A detailed, compliant quote for the treatment you need.

We coordinate it

A GP / medical letter

From a registered medical practitioner, confirming the treatment is medically necessary — to treat a life-threatening condition, or relieve acute or chronic pain, or a chronic mental condition.

We arrange it

A correct application

Submitted properly the first time, so it's not delayed or rejected over avoidable mistakes.

For your family too

Applying for a partner or child.

You can apply to release your super for a dependent's treatment — for example, a child's or partner's dental work — with evidence of your relationship and their condition. Many parents come to us for exactly this.

Check your situation

What you'd need for a dependent

  • Evidence of the relationship (e.g. child or partner).
  • Evidence of their dental condition and treatment need.
  • The same GP letter + itemised quote — we help organise both.
The honest bit

A few things we'll always be straight about.

— It's the ATO's call

Eligibility is ultimately decided by the ATO and your super fund. We never guarantee approval — but we'll give you an honest read before you commit.

— It's taxed

Early withdrawals are taxed (commonly ~17–22%) and reduce your retirement savings. We'll show you the numbers up front.

— It's a last-resort scheme

It's designed for when there's no other reasonable way to pay. If that's you, that's exactly who it's for.

Want to read the official rules yourself? We encourage it — alignment with the ATO is the whole point.
