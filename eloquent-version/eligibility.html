What's covered

Qualifying treatments

When it's medically necessary, a wide range of dental work can qualify. Purely cosmetic work generally doesn't — unless it restores genuine function.

The exception: cosmetic-looking work can qualify when it has a genuine medical purpose — like restoring function after an accident. Not sure where yours fits? That's what the free check is for.

The ATO's requirements

Three things, all handled

The ATO needs three things to approve a compassionate release. We gather every one of them for you.

An itemised dental quote

A detailed quote from your dentist setting out the treatment and the exact cost. We make sure it's compliant.

A letter from a GP

A registered medical practitioner confirming the treatment is medically necessary. We help arrange this.

A correct application

This is our part — preparing and lodging the ATO paperwork so it's complete, accurate, and far less likely to be delayed.

Applying for a dependent?

You can also apply to release your super for a dependent's treatment — a child or partner — with evidence of the relationship and the condition. We'll guide you through it.

The honest bit

A few things worth knowing upfront

It's taxed

Accessing super early is taxed, and the amount depends on your circumstances. The estimator above gives you a feel for it.

It's a last resort

CRS is for when there's no other reasonable way to pay. It reduces your retirement balance, so it's a real decision.

No guarantees

The ATO and your super fund decide. We can't promise approval — but we can give your application its best shot.

We're not financial advisers, and this isn't financial advice. For bigger decisions, it's wise to get independent advice too. You can read the official rules on the ATO website ↗.

Still not sure? Just ask.

A free, no-pressure chat is the surest way to know. If you're not eligible, we'll tell you honestly — we won't waste your time or your hope.
