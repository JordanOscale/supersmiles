In plain English

You may be eligible if…

No jargon, no fine print. If most of these sound like you, it's well worth a free check.

  • You've been told you need major dental work
  • You're in dental pain, discomfort or distress
  • You (or a dependent) have no other way to pay
  • You have some funds in your super
  • You can get a GP report, a dentist’s report, and an itemised dental quote (we help arrange all three)

Don't have a dentist or GP sorted? That's fine — we help with both.

What the ATO needs

What the ATO needs to assess it

The dental rules need two medical reports plus a quote. The reports and the treatment are your practitioners’ call — the admin is the part we help with.

1 · Two medical reports

A report from your GP and one from a registered dental practitioner — each confirming, in their own clinical judgement, that treatment is needed to relieve acute or chronic pain (or treat a life-threatening illness or injury).

2 · An itemised quote

A fully itemised quote from your dentist — each item, code and cost listed, on their letterhead, addressed to you and dated.

3 · A complete application

Everything assembled and lodged properly so it isn’t delayed or bounced back — the admin we help with. We don’t write the reports or decide your treatment.

Treatments

What usually qualifies — and what doesn't

Often qualifies

These may qualify only when genuinely needed to relieve pain or restore function:

  • Implants
  • Crowns
  • Oral surgery & extractions
  • Root canals
  • Reconstructive work — only when needed to relieve pain or restore function
Usually doesn't
  • Purely cosmetic veneers
  • Teeth whitening
  • Elective cosmetic-only work

Cosmetic work doesn’t become eligible just because it’s bundled with necessary treatment. The narrow exception is cosmetic work with a genuine medical purpose — like restoring function after trauma. Whether treatment is necessary is your dentist’s clinical call and the ATO’s decision, not ours.

For your family too

Applying for a partner or child.

You can apply to release your super for a dependent's treatment — for example, a child's or partner's dental work — with evidence of your relationship and their condition. Many parents come to us for exactly this.

Good to know

A couple of things worth knowing

Applying for a dependent

You can apply to release your super for a dependent's treatment — a child or partner — with evidence of the relationship and the condition. We'll tell you exactly what's needed.

We’re not tax advisers

We manage your application — not your finances. For anything about tax or your retirement savings, we’ll point you to a registered professional, and we’ll never pressure you into a decision. Accessing super early is a last resort — it’s worth first considering a payment plan with your dentist, savings, or other options. Early withdrawals are taxed and permanently reduce your retirement savings.

The program

ATO: Compassionate release of super

Super Smiles is a trading name of MEROITI, TUMAI (ABN 75 527 278 804). We help everyday Australians prepare and organise an application for the early release of super on compassionate grounds — the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) program — for dental treatment. We are not financial advisers, not the ATO, and not a tax or legal adviser; nothing on this site is financial, tax or legal advice. Eligibility and approval are decided solely by the ATO and your super fund — we cannot and do not guarantee any outcome. Accessing super early is taxed, reduces your retirement savings, and is intended as a last resort. Please consider your own circumstances and seek independent advice.
