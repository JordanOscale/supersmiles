What the ATO looks for

Three things generally need to be true

Every application is assessed individually, but eligibility usually comes down to these three pillars.

It's medically necessary

A registered practitioner supports that the treatment is needed to relieve pain, treat infection or prevent your health deteriorating.

You can't readily pay

The scheme is specifically for people who can't reasonably cover the cost of the treatment another way.

You have super to release

You hold an active Australian super balance large enough to cover the treatment and your fund's release minimum.

Treatment

What kind of dental work qualifies?

The deciding factor is health necessity. If treatment relieves pain or protects your health, it's far more likely to qualify than purely cosmetic work.

Typically qualifies
  • Dental implants to replace missing teeth
  • Root canal treatment for infection or pain
  • Crowns, bridges and major restorations
  • Treatment for moderate to severe gum disease
  • Complex extractions and oral surgery
  • Full-mouth rehabilitation where clinically needed
Usually won't qualify
  • Teeth whitening and bleaching
  • Veneers for purely aesthetic reasons
  • Cosmetic contouring or reshaping
  • Elective work with no health basis

"Means test" — reframed

Being asked whether you can afford the treatment isn't a judgment. This program was created specifically for people who can't readily cover necessary care — that's the whole point of it.

Remember the tax

Released super is taxed — typically around 22%, withheld before funds reach you, depending on your age and super components. Factor this in when estimating how much to release. We'll walk you through it.
